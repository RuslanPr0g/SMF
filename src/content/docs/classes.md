---
title: Object Classes
summary: Criteria governing the classification of specimens recorded at Site-Office.
order: 1
---

Classification describes one property only: whether the subject's behaviour changes under
instruction. It does not describe severity, frequency, or the observer's opinion of the
subject. A specimen that is merely unpleasant is not thereby reclassified.

Fixtures are not classified. A fixture cannot be instructed, so the question does not arise.

## Coachable

The subject's behaviour changes under instruction and the change persists into subsequent
rounds.

Criteria, all of which must be met:

1. Instruction is issued once and is not repeated.
2. The altered behaviour is observed in at least three subsequent rounds.
3. The alteration survives a lost round.

The third criterion is the one that fails. Reversion following a lost round is the most common
cause of a denied Coachable proposal. Nine specimens have been proposed for this class. One
holds it.

## Euclid

The subject's behaviour is understood but does not predict.

Applied where observation is sufficient to describe what the subject does and insufficient to
say when. Euclid is not a provisional class and carries no expectation of eventual
reclassification. A specimen may hold it permanently and most do.

Criteria:

1. Behaviour is documented across no fewer than twenty sessions.
2. No reliable trigger has been isolated.
3. Response to instruction is inconsistent rather than absent.

## Uninstall

The subject's behaviour is stable, understood, and does not change under instruction.

Criteria:

1. Behaviour is documented across no fewer than twenty sessions.
2. Instruction has been issued on no fewer than five occasions by separate parties.
3. No alteration has been observed following any such instruction.

The class is not a judgement of the subject. It is a statement about the utility of further
instruction, which is nil. Observers who continue to issue instruction to an Uninstall subject
are recorded as having done so.

## Reclassification

Any observer may submit a proposal. Proposals are reviewed against the criteria above and
against the session record. A proposal supported by fewer than twenty sessions is returned
without review.

Most proposals are denied. This is expected and is not a reflection on the submitting
observer.
